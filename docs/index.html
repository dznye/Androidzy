How to run Android apps on Windows 10 and 11

Four steps from download to your first app.

  1. Get Androidzy

    Download it from GitHub or build it yourself with one script. It is a single small .exe.

  2. First-run setup

    Accept Google's license and Androidzy fetches the emulator and Android 14 image (about 2 GB), or it reuses your Android Studio SDK.

  3. Press Launch

    Pick a GPU profile and a window size, then launch. Android boots in a normal window.

  4. Sign in and install

    Open Google Play, sign in and install your apps. Quick-boot keeps them ready for next time.

What you need

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit
  • Virtualization enabled in the BIOS
  • The Windows Hypervisor Platform feature
  • 8 GB of RAM (Android uses 4 GB by default)
  • About 6 GB of free disk space

Windows Subsystem for Android is gone. What now?

Microsoft ended support for Windows Subsystem for Android on March 5, 2025, which removed the built-in way to run Android apps on Windows 11. Phone Link can mirror a supported phone and Google Play Games on PC covers games. If you want general apps with Google Play on your PC, you need an emulator, and Androidzy is built for exactly that.

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers, including the limits.

Is there an Android emulator for PC that is made for apps, not games?

Androidzy is. It runs the official Google Android Emulator with Google Play on Windows, but it is set up for everyday apps: a clean Android 14 device that starts in seconds, stays signed in, and has no keymapping, macros or game store in the way.

How do I run Android apps on Windows 11 now that Windows Subsystem for Android is gone?

Microsoft ended support for Windows Subsystem for Android on March 5, 2025. Phone Link can mirror a supported phone and Google Play Games on PC covers games. For general apps you need an emulator: install Androidzy, accept Google's license on first run, sign in to Google Play and install your apps.

Can I use Androidzy as a second phone to keep work and personal accounts apart?

Yes. Androidzy gives you a separate Android 14 device on your PC with its own Google account, apps and data, so work chat, email and social accounts stay apart from your personal phone. Use it for accounts you own or are authorised to manage.

Can I sign in to multiple accounts or run multiple instances?

Within each app's own limits, yes. Instagram lets you add up to five accounts and Telegram supports several, and Android lets you add multiple Google accounts. Some services, such as WhatsApp, tie an account to a phone number, so a second account needs its own number. Androidzy currently runs one Android device at a time. Running several side by side is not supported yet.

Does Androidzy include Google Play?

It uses Google's official Android 14 system image with Google Play. Nothing from Google is bundled with Androidzy. On first run it downloads the emulator and image from Google's servers (about 2 GB) after you accept Google's license, or it reuses an Android Studio SDK you already have.

Will banking and streaming apps work in Androidzy?

Some may not. Banking, payment and DRM-protected streaming apps often check that they run on a certified physical phone and may block or limit any emulator. Androidzy does not try to hide that it is an emulator, so use a real phone for apps that require one.

Is Androidzy free and safe?

It is free. The launcher is open source under the MIT license, so you can read exactly what it does, and emulator usage metrics are switched off. The download is not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may show a warning. You can also build it yourself from source.

What do I need to run Androidzy?

Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), a CPU with virtualization turned on in the BIOS, the Windows Hypervisor Platform feature, 8 GB of RAM (4 GB goes to Android by default) and about 6 GB of free disk space.

Is Androidzy an alternative to BlueStacks, LDPlayer or MuMu?

For everyday apps, yes. Those players are built around games, with keymapping, macros and many simultaneous instances. Androidzy does less on purpose: one clean Android 14 device with GPU profiles. If you need keymapping or many instances at once, a gaming emulator fits better.
